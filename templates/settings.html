Email receipts

Finds the receipt emails that merchants such as Uber, Yango, food-delivery apps and online shops send you. Each receipt's items are added to the transaction it matches (same amount, within 3 days). A receipt from the last 30 days that matches no transaction is added as a new one, so a purchase shows up before your bank statement does. Uploading the statement later merges the two rather than counting the purchase twice. Items are read from the email itself, or from an attached PDF or photo.

This is the one feature that goes online.

It signs in to your mailbox to read receipts. It only reads: nothing is sent, moved, deleted or marked as read. Your password is used for this one scan and never saved. Only your email address is remembered, so you don't have to type it again. If a receipt is in another currency, the scan also fetches that day's exchange rate to convert it to kwacha. Only the currency code is sent, and the converted amount is replaced by your bank's figure when you upload the statement.

Not your normal password. For Gmail, turn on 2-Step Verification, then create one under Google Account → Security → App passwords.
